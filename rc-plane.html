1-metre RC airliner

An airliner-style RC aircraft with a 1-metre wingspan, designed and built from scratch before university: my first complete loop from CAD to printed parts to wired electronics.

  • SolidWorks
  • FDM printing
  • Print-parameter tuning
  • Soldering
  • Brushless + ESC
  • Servo linkages
In 60 seconds
  • Goal: a 1-metre wingspan airliner-style RC aircraft, designed and built from scratch.
  • Built: airframe modelled in SolidWorks and 3D-printed in sections over several iterations, with infill and print orientation tuned to balance strength against weight; I designed and soldered the electronics (brushless motor, ESC, servos, receiver, LiPo) and calculated the thrust-to-weight ratio.
  • Why it matters: my first complete product cycle (CAD → print → fit → revise → integrate electronics), done before university.

Design and build

The fuselage was printed in sections sized to the printer bed and revised several times: wall thickness against weight, how the sections joined, and where the servos, battery and wiring had to go. The control surfaces are driven through servo linkages I built; a brushless motor with an ESC and a LiPo pack provide propulsion, with the wiring and soldering done by hand. Print infill and orientation were tuned part by part to keep the structure stiff without adding weight.

Build video

What it taught me

Printed parts are only as good as the second and third iteration. Most of the project was measuring what did not fit, changing the model, and printing again, which is the same loop I now run on cryostat parts in the lab.
